# Reviewer guide

You are the last person before anything about a project becomes public. Two
things ask for your decision, and both arrive as email from GitHub. Each
takes minutes.

## 1. The publication request

Once a month the coordinator opens a **pull request** titled *Publish:
[month]*. A pull request is a proposed change with a diff: the exact text
that would change, line by line, and nothing else.

1. Click the link in the email, then **Files changed**.
2. For each record: is the project named correctly, are the figures the
   ones the project stands behind, is the story in the project's words with
   consent recorded, does every notice have its source, is the moku named,
   is there anything that should not be public?
3. Green checks at the bottom mean the automatic rules passed. A red check
   means the coordinator has more to do; you can stop there.
4. Decide:
   - **Review changes → Approve** — everything in it may publish.
   - **Review changes → Request changes** — say which record and why. The
     whole request returns to the coordinator; approved records are not
     lost, they wait for the next request.
   - To approve some records and not others, say so in the comment; the
     coordinator splits the request.

Your approval is recorded with your name and the time, permanently.

## 2. The deployment click

After the coordinator merges an approved request, the publishing job pauses
and emails you: **Review pending deployments**. Click **Approve and deploy**.
That click is the moment the public pages change. If you are not ready, do
nothing; nothing publishes until you are.

## Withdrawing something

If a published record must come down, tell the coordinator. They prepare a
pull request that moves it back; you approve it the same way, and the page
no longer shows it on the next build. The history keeps the record of why.

## What you never need to do

Edit records, run anything, or manage accounts.

## If something looks wrong

Open a **Correction** issue from the repository's Issues tab, or tell the
coordinator.
