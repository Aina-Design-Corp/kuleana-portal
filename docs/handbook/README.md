# The handbook — for the people who track the projects and tell their stories

Three short guides, one per role, written for the people now responsible
for keeping the Green Fee record: the project leads who report, the
coordinator who runs the monthly cycle, and the reviewers who decide what
publishes. Everything is done in a browser. Each guide fits on a page or
two when printed.

| Guide | Who it is for | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| [`project-lead-manual.md`](project-lead-manual.md) | Project leads, crew leads, and the people who report on a project's behalf | Reporting a cycle, adding a story or a photo, naming a place, what happens after you submit, what to do if a published fact is wrong |
| [`coordinator-runbook.md`](coordinator-runbook.md) | The coordinator | The monthly cycle in five sittings: notices and figures in, reports in, review, send for approval, follow up |
| [`reviewer-guide.md`](reviewer-guide.md) | Reviewers — the people who approve what publishes | The publication request, the deployment click, withdrawing something |

These guides use the record's own status words — `draft`, `validated`,
`published` — and explain each once, in plain words, where it first
appears. The rules behind them are in [`../../CONTRIBUTING.md`](../../CONTRIBUTING.md)
and, field by field, in Toolkit Document 2 on the site.

The same roles are taught as Moku Steward Training at
[Hui Koʻe ʻĀina](https://huikoeaina.ainadesign.org/). These guides are the
written half; the training is the practice.

Keeping the repository itself — who may change the rules and the checks,
how teams and protections are set — is in [`../ENTERPRISE.md`](../ENTERPRISE.md)
and `.github/CODEOWNERS`.
